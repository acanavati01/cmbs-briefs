1 big thing
Office CMBS delinquency hits all-time record as June maturity wall bites

Driving the news: The office CMBS delinquency rate hit 11.08% in June — a new all-time high per Trepp — as 2016-vintage 10-year loans matured with no viable refinancing path. Overall CMBS delinquency: 7.08% (+5 bps MoM). Special servicing rate: 10.30% (+13 bps).

By the numbers:

  • Office: 11.08% — all-time record  |  Retail: 10.82%
  • Overall: 7.08%  |  Multifamily: 6.57% ↓46 bps (bright spot)
  • SS rate: 10.30%; office loans = ~59% of new SS volume in 2026

Between the lines: Most new delinquencies are maturity defaults, not cash-flow failures. Morningstar DBRS pegs the 2026 CMBS maturity wall at $100B+, with more than half expected to miss repayment.

Sources: Trepp, KBRA, Morningstar DBRS

For dummies: More office loans are past-due than at any point in CMBS history — owners can't refinance when rates are this high — meaning the special servicer pipeline for office workouts is at a record backlog heading into 2H 2026.
Distressed CRE
$312M Lakewood Center mall matures — new buyer in workout talks

What's happening: The $312.6M CMBS loan on Lakewood Center (Lakewood, CA) hit its June maturity. Owner Pacific Retail Capital Partners — which bought the mall at ~half its original appraised value — is seeking an extension to sell land parcels. S&P and DBRS Morningstar downgraded several classes of DBWF 2015-LCN. No missed payments; 90%+ occupancy.

Sources: The Real Deal, CoStar, DBRS Morningstar

For dummies: A busy, performing mall can't refi because it's over-levered — the buyer bought cheap but still can't make the math work. The servicer can grant an extension (likely at a spread premium) or force a sale of an asset that actually has tenants. Extension is the better recovery trade here.
Macro
Fed holds; 10Y sticky at 4.42% — refi window stays shut

State of play: SOFR 3.60% · 10Y 4.42% · 30Y 4.93% · 2s/10s ~55 bps. Fed funds at 3.62%; July FOMC expected to hold. Futures have abandoned 2026 cut expectations and are pricing a possible hike if inflation stays sticky.

Why it matters: CMBS conduit 10-year fixed rates are running 5.50–7.10% — unworkable for any 2016 loan that priced sub-4%. Every additional month above 4.25% extends the maturity-default pipeline feeding special servicing.

Sources: Federal Reserve H.15, SOFR Rate, PrimeRates

For dummies: The rate cut that would bail out distressed borrowers isn't coming in 2026 — the Fed is frozen, so every maturing loan that can't afford today's rates is a new special servicing assignment.
Sponsors
Blackstone walks from Burbank office; doubles down on industrial

An $87M CMBS loan on a Blackstone/Worthe Burbank office asset transferred to SS at its June maturity with no refi update. Separately, Blackstone closed a $1B industrial portfolio refi — deliberate triage: shed legacy office, lever up logistics.

Source: Bisnow, CRE Daily

For dummies: Blackstone is surgically walking away from bad office while locking in cheap industrial debt — this is portfolio management, not financial distress. The servicer on the Burbank loan should expect a deed-in-lieu conversation, not a refi check.
👀 What I’m watching: CREFC June conference this week — servicer panels will signal whether the industry leans toward extend-and-pretend or accelerated enforcement on the maturity-default wave now cresting.
