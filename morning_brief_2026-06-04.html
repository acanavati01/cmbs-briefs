Texas CRE Foreclosure Auctions Hit $1.3B — Highest in Over a Year
Driving the news

Forty-eight CRE loans totaling $1.3 billion were flagged for June foreclosure auctions across the Texas Triangle — the highest monthly total since at least May 2025, per The Real Deal Texas. Eleven loans are in Dallas County alone.

By the numbers

Dominant property typeMultifamily

Nitya Capital / Palace Apts (Fort Worth)$28.2M loan
Repeat listings (ongoing negotiations)11 of 48
Between the lines

That 11-of-48 repeat rate signals borrowers and lenders are grinding through negotiations rather than resolving cleanly — expect continued extend-and-pretend before actual REO transfers.

Yes, but

Texas has no judicial foreclosure requirement; auctions can move fast. Not all flagged loans consummate — lenders routinely pull back at the courthouse steps after a last-minute deal.

For dummies: A wave of Texas apartment and hotel owners can't refinance or pay off their loans, so lenders are lining up to take the properties to auction — a leading indicator that more loans could land in special servicing or hit the REO bucket in Q3.
Sources: The Real Deal Texas (June 1, 2026); CRE Daily

By the numbers

  • Office delinquency: ~11.5% — near the Nov 2025 peak of 11.76%, worse than GFC
  • Retail special servicing rate: 10.82%
  • Multifamily delinquency: ~6.64% (broadening from 2021–22 vintage loans)
  • Overall special servicing rate: 8.23% (June 2026)
  • 345 office loans / $13.72B face maturity by year-end 2026
What's next

That $13.72B office maturity wall is the live threat for the rest of 2026. Expect a surge of transfer notices through Q3 as borrowers miss payoff dates with no viable refi.

For dummies: More than 1-in-8 office CMBS dollars are now delinquent — a level never seen before, even during the 2008 crisis — which means special servicers are fielding workouts on loans that have little realistic recovery path without deep discounts or foreclosure.
Sources: Trepp (April 2026); Multi-Housing News; S&P Global Ratings (March 2026)
Sponsor workout
Tishman Speyer Injects $20M to Extend $425M LIC Office CMBS Loan
What happened

Tishman Speyer avoided an imminent maturity default on its 1.2M SF Long Island City office complex (The JACX) by negotiating a 3-year extension (+ 1.5-year option) on the $425M CMBS loan that was due in September 2026. The sponsor injected $20M of fresh equity as part of the deal.

Why it matters

This is the playbook: large sponsor + trophy asset + equity injection = servicer grants time. Sets a benchmark for other office workout negotiations this year. The $20M equity cure also subordinates Tishman's position — aligning incentives to fill vacancy or sell.

For dummies: Tishman basically paid $20M to buy three more years to find tenants rather than hand the keys to the special servicer — a bet that LIC office demand recovers; for CWCAM, this is the mod structure to benchmark against for similar office consent requests.
Sources: Bisnow New York
