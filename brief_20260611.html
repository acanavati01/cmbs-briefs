1 big thing

Special Servicing Rate Hits 8.23% — Office & Retail Both Crack 10%

The CMBS special servicing rate climbed to 8.23% in June 2026 — its highest reading this cycle — as office (10.79%) and retail (10.82%) both cleared the 10% threshold simultaneously for the first time.

Why it matters: For CWCAM, a broader distress pool means more inbound transfer volume, longer resolution timelines, and growing exposure to dual-sector impairment. Deals from the 2019–2022 vintage with near-term maturities remain the highest-risk cohort.

By the numbers:

  • Retail SS rate: 10.82%
  • Office SS rate: 10.79%
  • Blended CMBS SS rate: 8.23%
  • REO share of distressed CMBS: 12.7% ($5.1B) — foreclosures where lenders now hold title
  • Overall CMBS distress rate (delinquent + SS): peaked at 12.07% in March; CRED iQ projects 15% by year-end

Sources: Trepp, CRE Daily, CRED iQ — June 2026

For dummies: More than 1 in 10 retail and office CMBS loans are now being managed by a special servicer — meaning borrowers couldn't keep up, the loans transferred to workout shops like CWCAM, and the resolution clock is ticking; expect heavier caseload and more REO conversions through year-end.

Capital markets

Fresh $633.5M Conduit Prices Tight — AAA at J+66.8

Driving the news: KBRA assigned preliminary ratings to BBCMS 2026-5C42, a $633.5M conduit backed by 37 loans across 58 properties in 15 MSAs. The deal priced the AAA class at J+66.8 (yield 4.73%), settling on or about June 24.

Between the lines: Conduit execution holding at sub-70 bps on the AAA — despite aggregate distress north of 12% — signals that senior bond buyers are still comfortable with structural subordination. The highest concentrations: industrial (26.7%), lodging (19.1%), mixed-use (14.3%).

Yes, but: Q1 2026 total issuance was $32.74B (-12.8% YoY) across 42 deals; SASB dominated at $24.54B. Volume compression is real even as execution is stable.

Sources: KBRA, SEC EDGAR FWP filings — BBCMS 2026-5C42 (June 2026)

For dummies: A new $633M bond deal backed by commercial loans just priced at fairly tight spreads, meaning investors are still buying CMBS at decent prices — that's good news for originators and keeps the securitization pipeline open, but annual volume is running below last year's pace.

Distress watch

Cincinnati Office Towers Head to Sheriff's Auction June 17

Driving the news: Two downtown Cincinnati office towers owned by Philadelphia-based Rubenstein Partners — 26-story 312 Elm St. and 15-story 312 Plum St. — are listed for Hamilton County Sheriff's Office auction on June 17. Separately, JLL is marketing Two North LaSalle (CSMC 2007-C2), a 691K SF, 26-story Class B office in the Chicago Loop that has been REO since October 2024.

What's next: The Cincinnati auction outcome will set a public price signal for secondary Midwest office — watch for bids at 30–50 cents on the dollar. Two North LaSalle is one of the larger active CMBS REO dispositions; a sale will trigger principal loss allocation back to the trust.

Sources: The Real Deal, Connect CRE — June 2026

For dummies: Two Cincinnati office buildings are going to a forced auction next week, and a 691,000 SF Chicago tower is already bank-owned and on the market — both are real-world examples of what happens at the end of a CMBS workout when a borrower can't pay and the lender takes the keys; sale prices will reset local comps and determine how much the bond trust gets back.

Macro + sector

Rates Steady; Multifamily Vacancy Tightens to 4.8%

By the numbers (as of June 9):

  • 10Y Treasury: 4.56%  |  2Y: 4.15%  |  30Y: 5.03%
  • Multifamily vacancy: 4.8% (Q1 2026, CBRE) — down; avg rent $2,217/mo (+0.2% YoY)
  • Small MF cap rates compressed to 5.8% in Q1 from 6.1% — lowest since early 2024
  • CMBS loan rates (10-yr fixed): 5.50–7.10% depending on property type

The bottom line: The rate curve is holding flat — no near-term refi relief for distressed office/retail borrowers. Multifamily's tightening vacancy is a bright spot that should support Fannie/Freddie collateral performance.

Sources: Federal Reserve H.15, CBRE Q1 2026 Multifamily Figures, PeerSense — June 2026

For dummies: The 10-year Treasury is stuck near 4.56%, which means borrowing costs stay high and struggling office/retail borrowers still can't refinance their way out of trouble — apartments are a different story, with vacancies dropping and rents holding, which keeps Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac multifamily loans healthier.
What I'm watching: The Hamilton County sheriff's auction on June 17 — bid depth and price discovery on the Cincinnati office towers will be the first clean data point on Midwest CBD office liquidation value in 2026, directly informing reserve-setting and loss-projection conversations on comparable REO in the CWCAM portfolio.
