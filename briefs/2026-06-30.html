1 Big Thing
Fed flips hawkish — rate hike, not cut, now the base case for 2026
At its June 17 FOMC meeting, the Federal Reserve held at 3.50–3.75% for the fourth consecutive time — and revised its inflation forecast sharply higher. The shift from "patient hold" to implicit tightening bias is the single most important macro development for CMBS pricing right now.

By the numbers:

  • Headline inflation revised to 3.6% (from 2.7% projected in March); core to 3.3%
  • 9 of 18 FOMC officials now pencil in at least one rate hike in 2026
  • Median year-end fed funds projection: 3.8% — markets had priced 1–2 cuts
  • Next meeting: July 28–29 (no SEP; no Powell press conference signal)

Why it matters: CMBS bridge loans and construction loans price off SOFR — any hike adds 25 bps directly to floating-rate debt service on already-stressed borrowers. Extension mods negotiated at today's SOFR + 350–450 bps could blow through DSCR covenants. Watch for a new wave of maturity default triggering events in Q3.

Between the lines: The 10Y is already at 4.40% — stubbornly above the level that would allow conduit underwriting to pencil at sub-7% coupons. Expect cap rate expansion pressure to persist, especially on office and retail assets where exit assumptions are already stretched.

For dummies:

The Fed is now leaning toward raising rates, not cutting them — which means borrowers hoping to refi their way out of special servicing are stuck even longer, and any extension mod you approve today needs bigger interest rate cushion built in.

Sources: Federal Reserve FOMC Statement June 17, 2026; Forbes Fed Meeting Tracker; Chatham Financial US Market Rates
Distressed CRE
Two notable loans hit special servicing as GSA exposure bites

Driving the news:

  • 425 Eye Street, Washington DC — 02.2M CMBS loan transferred for imminent monetary default. 64% GSA-tenanted office building with the majority of government leases expiring June 2026. Classic GSA cliff risk.
  • Simon Premium Outlets — 3.9M CMBS loan missed its June 2026 maturity date, triggering special servicing transfer. Three tertiary-market outlet centers (MA, SC, GA); cash flow is breakeven; short-term extension likely.

Yes, but: The Simon outlets deal is a liquidity/maturity default, not a cash-flow default — more resolvable. Eye Street is more complex: government lease rollover in the current federal footprint-reduction environment makes re-tenanting speculative.

The bottom line: GSA exposure is officially a top-tier risk flag for surveillance. Any CMBS office collateral with >20% GSA tenancy approaching a lease expiration should be on active watchlist.

For dummies:

A DC office building lost its main government tenant and can't pay its loan — a warning shot for any CW loan backed by federal office tenants. Simon's mall loan is just late to refinance, not fundamentally broken.

Sources: Connect CRE Return to Lender (week of June 25, 2026); Commercial Loan Direct Special Servicing Guide 2026
CMBS Mechanics
Special servicing climbs to 8.23%; office and retail both breach 10%

By the numbers:

  • Overall special servicing rate: 8.23% (Trepp, June 2026)
  • Retail SS rate: 10.82% | Office SS rate: 10.79%
  • Overall CMBS delinquency: 7.55% (April/May; June release pending)
  • AA spread widened 10 bps; A spread widened 10 bps vs. last print

What's next: KBRA forecasts full-year 2026 private-label issuance of 83B — a post-GFC record — even as distress metrics remain elevated. YTD issuance through May already hit 6.7B (+9.7% YoY). The duality of strong issuance + rising distress reflects a bifurcated market: new-vintage, well-underwritten collateral prints fine; legacy office/retail drags the loss side.

For dummies:

More than 1 in 10 office and retail CMBS loans are now in special servicing — your pipeline of active assets should only get busier, especially on anything originated 2018–2022 that hasn't matured yet.

Sources: Trepp CMBS Special Servicing June 2026; KBRA 2026 U.S. CMBS Outlook; S&P Global CMBS Delinquency Brief May 2026
Sponsors & Capital Markets
Blackstone's Chicago tower defaults; Macerich, SL Green diverge

Driving the news:

  • Blackstone Mortgage Trust / 601W: 43M loan on One South Wacker Drive (40-story, West Loop) defaulted on its June 9 maturity. Property is 73% occupied. BXMT confirmed it was on watchlist since 2022; exposure is <2% of REIT portfolio. *(Bloomberg, June 24)*
  • Macerich: 6.5M Twenty Ninth Street loan in default (effective Feb. 2026); company launched 14M share offering June 15 — dilutive move signals balance sheet stress.
  • SL Green: 100% occupancy at 500 Park Ave; sold 10 E. 53rd St. to Meadow Partners for 12.2M, generating ~00M net for debt repayment. Manhattan prime office: 2.9% vacancy.

Between the lines: SL Green's asset sale strategy (de-lever via dispositions) is the model for surviving this cycle. Macerich's equity dilution path is riskier. For CMBS servicers, track any borrower that is equity-raising — it often precedes a workout ask.

For dummies:

A big Chicago office tower just failed to pay off its loan; separately, one mall REIT is selling stock to raise cash while another is selling buildings at a profit — the haves and have-nots of CRE are getting further apart.

Sources: Bloomberg June 24, 2026; Bisnow Chicago; SEC EDGAR Macerich 8-K; Bisnow NY Deal Sheet
