1 Big Thing

Texas CRE Foreclosures Hit Record $1.3B in June

Dallas-area distress is driving the highest single-month foreclosure auction volume since tracking began, with multifamily leading the collapse alongside a major hotel default.

Why it matters: The Sun Belt overbuilding + rate-cap expiration + bridge-loan maturity vortex is fully in motion. Texas is the sharpest leading indicator for what arrives next in the broader CMBS multifamily and hospitality books.

By the numbers:

  • $1.3B in loans flagged for June auction — 48 loans, all-time record
  • $172M — The Line Austin hotel (428 keys, 111 Cesar Chavez St.) defaulted
  • $78.6M — S2 Capital’s The Republic Apartments (1,033 units, Garland, TX) at risk
  • $70M distress patch — Nitya Capital faces foreclosure on 3 DFW complexes (847 units; lender: One William Street Capital Mgmt)

Between the lines: Dallas (11 loans), Collin County (9), and Tarrant County (10) dominate the docket. Nitya Capital, which closed a blockbuster refi deal just last year, now faces simultaneous foreclosures — a sign that 2024’s extend-and-pretend refis are themselves maturing into distress.

For dummies:

Scores of Texas apartment and hotel owners who borrowed at near-zero rates can’t pay off or refinance now — their buildings are heading to courthouse steps, which feeds more REO and workout volume directly into special servicer pipelines.

Sources: The Real Deal Texas (June 1–2, 2026)
CMBS Distress

Mall Loans Sour, May Delinquency Climbs to $45.67B

CMBS delinquent volume (30+ days) rose $646M (+1.44%) to $45.67B in May, driven by sizable shopping mall loan failures.

What’s next: The $312.6M Lakewood Center mall loan — transferred in January for imminent maturity default — hits its due date this month. A mod/extension or liquidation call is imminent and will be a closely-watched precedent.

Yes, but: The MoM increase was modest; the more alarming read is the March spike (+41bps, biggest in 3 years) that reset the base, with May now extending above it.

For dummies:

The pile of past-due CMBS loans keeps growing, mostly driven by malls that can’t refinance — more files heading to special servicers this summer, and Lakewood Center’s resolution will set a tone for big-box retail workouts.

Sources: Commercial Real Estate Direct / Trepp (June 2, 2026)
Special Servicing

SS Rate Near 12-Year High; Office Drives Half of New Transfers

CMBS special servicing rate climbed to ~11% in March (up 27bps); office and multifamily each added 44–45bps that month. Retail’s SS rate stood at 11.76% in January. April slowed the pace of new transfers — but volume still grew.

By the numbers:

  • Office loans: >50% of March’s $2.9B in new SS transfers (42 loans)
  • CRED iQ projects overall distress rate stays 11–12.5% through mid-2026
  • Primary transfer trigger: maturity defaults, not payment defaults
For dummies:

About 1 in 9 CMBS loans is now with a special servicer — near a 12-year high — meaning workout calendars stay packed with no clear relief until at least Q4.

Sources: Trepp / CRED iQ / Connect CRE (2026)
Macro / Rates

Rates Anchored Ahead of June 16–17 FOMC

By the numbers:

  • 10Y Treasury: 4.46%  •  30Y: 4.98%
  • 2Y: 4.04%  •  SOFR: 3.65%
  • ~30% market-implied probability of a hike by Q1 2027; no cut expected at June meeting

The bottom line: With SOFR anchored at 3.65% and the 10Y above 4.40%, debt service on floating-rate CMBS loans remains punishing. No Fed relief on the near-term horizon — cap rate compression not in play; workouts remain the primary resolution path.

For dummies:

High rates mean distressed CMBS borrowers still can’t refinance their way out — the only exits are mods, note sales, or REO dispositions, all of which land on a special servicer’s desk.

Sources: Federal Reserve H.15 / CME FedWatch (June 1, 2026)
