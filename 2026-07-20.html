The Canavati Brief — Monday, July 20, 2026

1 big thing: Delinquency rate falls, but the mix underneath is ugly

Trepp's June read: overall CMBS delinquency fell 20bp to 7.35%, dragged down almost entirely by lodging (-79bp to 5.22%). Office and retail kept climbing.

By the numbers (June 2026, Trepp):

SectorRateChg (bp)
Office11.57%+4
Multifamily7.23%+28
Retail6.91%+30
Lodging5.22%-79
Industrial1.20%-11

Yes but: counting loans current on interest but past maturity, the rate is 9.53% — up 36bp — the real pipeline headed for servicers.

Source: Trepp, MBA Newslink

For dummies: the headline number looks better because hotels recovered, but office and retail are quietly getting worse, and a growing stack of "current but unpaid at maturity" loans is the real workout queue building behind the scenes.

Harborplace mall back in special servicing

  • The CMBS loan on Baltimore's Inner Harbor Harborplace transferred to Midland again, with payoff at maturity unlikely.
  • The asset previously cycled through servicing in 2018-19, ending in a court-appointed receiver.

Source: Commercial Real Estate Direct

For dummies: a marquee Baltimore mall's loan is in trouble again — proof that a workout isn't finished until the loan actually pays off or the asset trades, not just when servicing ends.

CRE CLO issuance humming, spreads steady

  • CRE CLO AAA/BBB- spreads held flat at +145/+150 (static/managed) and +350/+360; conduit BBB- unchanged at +450.
  • Broader CLO issuance hit $126.5B in Q2 as the market rebounded from an April slowdown.

Source: PitchBook, CRE Daily

For dummies: cheap short-term financing is still flowing to bridge lenders — good for liquidity, but managed CRE CLOs carry more extension risk if underlying loans slip.

Maturity wall: extend-and-pretend runs out of road

  • $875B in CRE loans (17% of outstanding CRE debt) mature in 2026, swollen by loans already extended once in 2024-25.
  • 10Y Treasury sits at 4.55%; FOMC (July 28-29) expected to hold — no rate relief on the horizon.

Source: CoStar, CBRE

For dummies: the loans everyone kicked down the road are now due, and there's no rate cut coming to bail them out — expect more transfers to special servicing, not fewer.

What I'm watching: whether the July 28-29 FOMC meeting moves rate-cut odds enough to ease 2026 refi math, or whether extended loans finally convert to REO.
