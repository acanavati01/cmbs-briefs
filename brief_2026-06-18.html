Warsh's Debut: Fed Signals Rate-Hike Risk, Drops Cut Guidance

New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh held rates at 3.5%–3.75% on June 17 — but scrapped forward guidance on cuts and revamped the statement to be dramatically shorter. The median dot plot now projects the fed funds rate at 3.8% by year-end 2026, up from 3.4% in March — meaning 18 of 19 participants see at least one hike as necessary this year.

Why it matters

The 10Y Treasury sits at 4.461%. A rate hike would push cap rates higher and close the refi window further for the $100B+ in CMBS loans maturing in 2026 — more than half of which DBRS estimates will fail to repay at maturity.

2Y Treasury4.052%
2s/10s spread+41bps

Median 2026 dot3.8% (was 3.4% in March)
Between the lines

Warsh withheld his own dot-plot entry and said he'd form task forces to overhaul Fed operations — a signal the new regime is still calibrating. Markets are pricing near-zero probability of a July cut.

Sources: Federal Reserve H.15; CNBC, NPR, Kiplinger (June 17, 2026)

For dummies: The new Fed chair just replaced "we'll probably cut" with "we might hike." Every distressed-loan workout model in your portfolio that assumed lower rates by Q3 needs a fresh look.

Capital Markets

KBRA Joins Bloomberg CMBS Index; Agency Issuance Up 43% YTD

What's new

Effective with the June 2026 month-end Bloomberg index rebalance, KBRA ratings will be incorporated into Bloomberg's CMBS index methodology — expanding eligible buyers to index-tracking funds that previously required S&P/Moody's/Fitch ratings.

By the numbers
  • Agency CMBS YTD issuance: $38.4B (+43% vs. same period 2025)
  • Conduit AAA: +78bps | A-S: +105bps | BBB-: +450bps
  • SASB AAA: +108–177bps (range by property type)
  • Recent deals: BBCMS 2026-5C42 ($633.5M), Benchmark 2026-B43

Sources: KBRA, Morningstar, CRE Daily Outlook 2026

For dummies: KBRA-rated CMBS deals can now flow into Bloomberg index funds — more demand for KBRA paper, which could tighten spreads and make new issuance easier to execute. Good for refi liquidity, bad for your yield.
