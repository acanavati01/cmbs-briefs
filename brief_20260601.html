20 Times Square ($647.5M) Returns to Special Servicing

Maefield Development's Times Square Trust 2018-20TS — a $647.5M single-borrower CMBS deal originated by Natixis — missed its May 2026 maturity date and transferred back to special servicing. This is the property's second stint in SS in four years.

Why it matters
  • The loan first entered SS in November 2022 and received a modification/extension in late 2023 — that runway has now expired.
  • Collateral is a 99-year ground lease (runs through 2117) supporting a 454-room Marriott Edition hotel and four floors of retail space that remains largely vacant.
  • The ground lease structure limits recovery options: no fee interest to sell, making a UCC foreclosure or deed-in-lieu the most likely path.
By the numbers
  • $647.5M outstanding CMBS debt (Times Square Trust 2018-20TS)
  • 2nd special servicing transfer in 4 years
  • 454-room hotel + 4 floors of largely vacant retail
  • Ground lease term: through 2117
Between the lines

The 2023 extension effectively bought 18–24 months. With retail vacancy unchanged and hotel performance under pressure, the path to a payoff at par looks narrow. Expect the SS to weigh a note sale vs. a negotiated modification round 2 — but the ground lease makes a traditional foreclosure slow and expensive.

For dummies: A giant $647.5M Times Square hotel/retail loan that already needed rescuing two years ago just missed its due date again — the special servicer now has to decide whether to offer the borrower yet another extension, sell the distressed note, or start the legal process to take over a complicated ground-lease property.

Sources: Commercial Observer, The Real Deal, Hoodline (May 2026)

KBRA Joins Bloomberg CMBS Index at Month-End

KBRA becomes the fourth rating agency incorporated into Bloomberg Index Services' CMBS index methodology, effective the June 30, 2026 month-end rebalance.

Driving the news
  • The Bloomberg CMBS index covers 2,300+ securities totaling ~$447B in market value (as of March 2026).
  • KBRA joins Moody's, Fitch, and S&P in a rules-based multi-agency framework.
  • Index eligibility recalibrates: securities rated exclusively by KBRA — or where KBRA is the controlling rating — may now qualify for index inclusion.
For dummies: Starting June 30, the giant bond-index scoreboard that big institutional funds track will start counting KBRA's grades — potentially opening index-eligible buying for KBRA-rated CMBS deals that were previously excluded.

Source: KBRA (May 2026)

Trepp: Office Delinquency at Historic 12.34%; Overall Rate 7.55%

March 2026 Trepp data shows overall CMBS delinquency rebounding to 7.55% (+41 bps from February's brief dip to 7.14%), with office at a cycle-high 12.34% — above every reading from the 2008–2010 GFC.

By the numbers
  • Overall CMBS delinquency: 7.55% (Mar 2026) vs. 7.14% (Feb) vs. 7.47% (Jan)
  • Office CMBS: 12.34% — a new all-time high, 1.6 pp above GFC peak
  • Mall delinquency: 11.2%  |  Multifamily: 6.64%
  • 345 office loans / $13.72B face year-end 2026 maturity
Yes, but

Strong new CMBS issuance continues to dilute the headline delinquency rate — the dollar volume of distressed loans is rising faster than the percentage suggests.

For dummies: More than 1-in-8 office CMBS loans are now past-due — worse than the financial crisis — and nearly $14B more will hit their maturity dates before year-end with nowhere to refinance.

Sources: Trepp via MBA Newslink, S&P Global SF Credit Brief (Q1 2026)

Rates Steady: 10Y at 4.42%, Fed Holds

By the numbers
  • 10Y UST: 4.42%  |  30Y UST: 4.93%
  • SOFR: ~3.65% (anchored to Fed funds)
  • Fed funds target: 3.50%–3.75% — unchanged since Dec 11, 2025
  • CME FedWatch: ~85% probability of hold at next FOMC
What's next

No cut is priced until at least late Q3 2026. For borrowers facing 2026 maturities, cap rates remain inverted vs. financing costs in most office and retail submarkets.

For dummies: The Fed isn't cutting rates anytime soon, which means distressed borrowers can't refinance their way out of trouble — keeping the special servicer pipeline full.

Sources: Federal Reserve H.15, CME FedWatch (May 29, 2026)
