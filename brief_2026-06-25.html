$290M Broadway Office Loan Transferred to KeyBank Special Servicing

Driving the news: The $290 million CMBS mortgage on 26 Broadway — an 839,712 sq ft office tower in Lower Manhattan — was flagged in TreppWire this week as facing a cash-flow crunch and an expected default, triggering a transfer to KeyBank as special servicer. The loan is split across three tranches, with the largest note at $222.2M.

  • Property is a Lower Manhattan trophy-adjacent office with heavy 2016-vintage CMBS exposure
  • Transfer signals imminent payment or maturity default; exact maturity date not confirmed in available sources
  • Lower Manhattan office vacancy remains elevated; Brookfield's One NYP (one block away) recently required a $25M paydown and $20M leasing reserve to earn a 2-year extension

By the numbers: Trepp CMBS office delinquency: 11.53% in May 2026 (−16 bps MoM but still near post-GFC cycle high). Overall SS rate: 8.23% (office sector SS: 10.79%).

For dummies: A $290M office loan just landed on a special servicer's desk — that servicer now controls strategy (mod, foreclosure, note sale). For CWCAM: if this is in a pool you surveil, review the DSCR trend and watch for B-note/mez holders with blocking rights before the SS proposes a workout strategy.
Sources: Commercial Real Estate Direct (June 23, 2026); TreppWire; Trepp May 2026 delinquency report
Big Manhattan Multifamily Loan Resolves, Shrinks SS Volume

Driving the news: A large Manhattan apartment loan in CMBS special servicing was resolved as of June 24, with CRE News reporting the payoff or cure will meaningfully reduce the overall SS volume on CMBS balance sheets.

Why it matters: Multifamily SS rate dropped 57 bps to 8.51% in May (Trepp), and the delinquency rate fell 76 bps to 6.95% — the most improved sector month-over-month. High-supply markets (Austin, Charlotte, Phoenix) are still pressuring newer vintage loans.

For dummies: A big apartment loan just paid off or cured — good news if you hold that bond, and a reminder that multifamily distress is resolving faster than office right now. Sponsors with equity are finding ways out; the hard cases are the ones without it.
Source: Commercial Real Estate Direct (June 24, 2026); Trepp May 2026
Brookfield Earns 2-Year Mod on $835M One NYP Loan

Between the lines: After entering special servicing in January 2026, Brookfield secured a two-year extension on the $835M CMBS loan backing One New York Plaza, pushing maturity to January 2028. KBRA is monitoring the modification.

  • Required: $25M upfront principal paydown (balance now ~$810M)
  • Required: $20M deposit into leasing reserve; replenished $20M per extension option
  • Two additional 1-year options available if no default
  • Cash flow sweep in place throughout term

Yes but: Extension buys time, not a solution. Brookfield must show leasing velocity at One NYP or face a harder conversation in 2028 with less market patience.

For dummies: Brookfield paid $25M to buy two more years to lease up a big downtown office tower. The servicer got cash, a leasing war chest, and a cash sweep — a textbook "extend and amend" with real teeth. Watch whether the leasing reserve actually gets deployed.
Sources: Bisnow; KBRA surveillance note; Commercial Real Estate Direct (Mar 4, 2026)
10Y Treasury Slips Below 4.45% on Oil/Geopolitical Shift

Driving the news: The 10-year Treasury yield fell to 4.406% on June 24 — down roughly 9 bps from prior session — as Brent crude dropped to pre-Iran-war levels, easing inflation risk sentiment. SOFR holds at 3.62%; no Fed move expected before September at earliest per CME FedWatch.

Why it matters for CRE: Every 10-bp rally in the 10Y is roughly 10 bps of spread compression on new CMBS pricing. The SL Green One Madison SASB deal in March printed at T+181 (5.81%) — a similar deal today would price closer to 5.71–5.75% all-in, improving refi economics modestly for well-occupied assets.

For dummies: Rates ticked down yesterday, which is a small win for borrowers trying to refinance. It doesn't fix the office problem, but it slightly widens the window for assets with real cash flow to pencil out on a refi.
Sources: CNBC (June 24, 2026); Federal Reserve H.15; CME FedWatch; SOFR Rate (NY Fed)
