1 big thing

Rate hike priced in by year-end — maturity wall just got harder

A blowout May nonfarm payrolls print and sticky 4.2% CPI have flipped the rate narrative: futures markets now fully price a Fed rate hike by December 2026, not the cuts borrowers were counting on. The 10-year Treasury sits at 4.52%; SOFR at 3.63%.

Why it matters:

The 2026 CRE maturity wall hits ~62B — up 56% from 2025. "Extend and pretend" loans that rolled into this window were underwritten on a rate-cut glide path. That path just closed.

By the numbers:

  • 10Y Treasury: 4.52%  |  SOFR: 3.63%
  • May CPI: 4.2% YoY (core +0.2% MoM, in-line)
  • Blowout payrolls → Dec 2026 hike fully priced by futures
  • CRE payoff/refi rate: 78% in Q1 2026 — but declining on office & retail
For dummies: Interest rates just got worse, not better. Every borrower who extended their loan hoping to refi at lower rates is now more likely to default — and land on your desk.

Sources: Federal Reserve H.15, CME FedWatch, CRE Daily, Trepp

Distressed workout

Aon Center's $536M loan heads for July cliff

601W Cos. will not repay the $536M CMBS loan (JPMDB 2018-C8) on Chicago's 2.8M SF Aon Center at its July 2026 maturity. The loan re-entered special servicing in February after the borrower missed a required $2.5M TI/LC payment — its second stint in SS following a 2023 modification and 3-year extension.

Between the lines:

Occupancy is 66%, down from 88% at origination. A previously-granted extension has expired with no visible exit. Foreclosure or deed-in-lieu appears the likely path.

For dummies: Chicago's biggest office tower borrowed $536M, tried to buy time in 2023, ran out of it, and now the servicer will have to decide between taking the keys or a fire-sale note sale.

Sources: Commercial Real Estate Direct, The Real Deal Chicago, Bisnow

Retail SS

Lakewood Center's $309M loan: new JV, same problem

Pacific Retail Venture — which acquired the 2M SF Lakewood Center mall in Lakewood, CA from Macerich — is in active workout negotiations on the $309M CMBS loan that matured in June 2026. The loan (DBWF 2015-LCN) moved to SS in January 2026 and S&P / DBRS Morningstar have already downgraded multiple classes.

Yes but:

The buyer has a concrete mixed-use redevelopment plan — shopping, dining, residential — which gives the SS a viable exit narrative absent in most distressed mall workouts.

For dummies: New owners bought a struggling SoCal mall and now need to negotiate with CMBS bondholders to restructure a past-due loan before they can start converting it into apartments and retail.

Sources: The Real Deal LA, CRE News, CoStar

Capital markets

BBCMS 5C42 priced; KBRA joins Bloomberg CMBS index

KBRA assigned preliminary ratings to BBCMS 2026-5C42, a $633.5M conduit backed by 37 loans across 58 properties in 15 MSAs — the latest in a steady Q2 pipeline. Separately, KBRA's ratings were incorporated into the Bloomberg CMBS Index methodology effective June 2026, broadening investor use of the agency's surveillance data.

By the numbers:

  • Q1 2026 domestic CMBS issuance: $32.74B across 42 deals (−12.8% YoY)
  • SASB = 74% of Q1 volume ($24.54B, 32 deals)
  • CMBS SS rate (June): 8.23%; Office: 10.79%; Retail: 10.82%

What's next:

KBRA projects record full-year 2026 issuance but more downgrades than upgrades in H2 — driven by office and emerging multifamily distress.

For dummies: New CMBS deals keep getting done because investors still want the yield, but the rating agencies expect to cut more ratings than they raise for the rest of the year as loans continue to go bad.

Sources: KBRA, CRE Direct, Trepp, Bloomberg

What I'm watching:

Whether 601W Cos. files for deed-in-lieu or forces JPMDB 2018-C8 bondholders into a protracted Aon Center foreclosure — a $536M loss realization on a July maturity will set Chicago office SS comps and signal whether major workout concessions are back on the table.
