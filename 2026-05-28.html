1 big thing
30-Year Treasury Cracks 5% — CMBS Refinance Math Breaks

The 30-year Treasury closed Tuesday at 5.01%; the 10-year at 4.48%. At these levels, exit cap rates must clear ~6.5%+ on most CMBS collateral to support new debt. Most sponsors who've been extending since 2022 can't clear that bar without a material paydown or fresh equity.

Why it matters

Every 25bp rise in the 10Y adds roughly –6/sf in annual debt service on a standard deal. The long end has now been above 4.4% for weeks — not a spike, a new floor. SOFR at ~3.65% keeps floating-rate pain elevated too.

The bottom line

A 5%+ 30-year is a go/no-go gate for most CRE refis right now. June and July maturities hitting with no viable exit will accelerate involuntary SS transfers through Q3.

Source: Federal Reserve H.15, May 27, 2026; CME FedWatch

For dummies: When long-term rates stay this high, refinancing a maturing CMBS loan is nearly impossible — so borrowers who can't pay off just transfer to special servicing, adding to the workout queue.
Multifamily CMBS Spikes 56 bps — Now 7.71%

Trepp's April delinquency rate was nearly flat overall at 7.54% (−1 bp), masking a sharp multifamily surge: up 56 bps to 7.71% — a new high — driven by two large loans in San Francisco and New York City going 30-day delinquent.

By the numbers
  • Office: 11.69% (↓2 bps — mods/extensions concealing stress)
  • Multifamily: 7.71% (↑56 bps — new watch list)
  • Lodging: 6.52% (↓79 bps)
  • Retail: 6.31% (↓31 bps)
  • Industrial: 0.96%
Between the lines

Office mods suppress the headline number but just push the maturity wall to 2027–28, when rates may or may not be friendlier. The One New York Plaza (35M) mod extended it to January 2028.

Source: Trepp CMBS Delinquency Report, April 2026; MBA Newslink

For dummies: Big urban apartment loans are now going sideways alongside office — special servicers should expect multifamily in their queue, not just downtown towers.
Chicago's Aon Center: 36M CMBS Ticking Toward July

601W Cos.' 36M senior CMBS loan on Chicago's Aon Center returned to special servicing in February after a missed payment — its second trip in three years. Occupancy has fallen to 66% (from 88% at 2018 origination). Servicer commentary: repayment at July 2026 maturity is "unlikely." Subordinate mezzanine debt complicates any resolution.

What's next

Midland Loan Services must choose: another mod/extension, discounted payoff, or enforcement — with senior/mezz creditor conflict making a clean outcome difficult before year-end.

Source: The Real Deal Chicago; Bisnow; CRE News, March 2026

For dummies: A half-empty Chicago skyscraper with layered debt and no refi path means the servicer is almost certainly going to own this problem through year-end — textbook "extend until you can't."
Lakewood Center Mall (12.6M) Into SS Pre-Maturity

The Pacific Retail Capital Partners / Lyon Living / Silverpeak JV's 12.6M loan on Lakewood Center (Lakewood, CA) transferred to special servicing for imminent maturity default ahead of its June 2026 due date. No refi plan was presented. DSCR: 1.33x. Occupancy: 93%.

Yes, but

The property still cash flows — this is a capital structure failure, not an operational one. A mod or short extension is achievable if the trust is willing.

Source: BBG; CRE Daily; Commercial Real Estate Direct

For dummies: Even a healthy, 93%-occupied mall can hit special servicing when today's rates make refinancing impossible — proof that the distress wave isn't just about bad assets, it's about bad timing.
